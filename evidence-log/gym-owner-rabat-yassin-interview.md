## 1. Purpose

To learn which of a real Rabat studio's classes is emptiest, what the owner has done to fill it, and whether he would open 2 spots in that class to new people only, at his normal price.

## 2. Participant profile

Karim owns an independent yoga and mobility studio in Rabat running about 16 classes a week. He sets the schedule himself and follows attendance class by class.

## 3. Summary of answers

| # | Question | Answer |
|---|---|---|
| Q1 | Emptiest class and attendance | Friday at 12:30, usually 2 people (sometimes 3) out of 8 mats. At that hour people are at the mosque or at lunch with family. |
| Q2 | What he has tried to fill it | Only a few Instagram posts. He thought about cancelling the class, but 2 regulars only come on that slot, so he keeps running it and pays the teacher anyway. |
| Q2 follow-up | Lowering the price | No. Once the price drops, everyone wants the lower price, and his regulars talk to each other. |
| Q3 | Open 2 spots to new people only, at normal price? What would stop him? | Maybe. It depends on who controls it. He must choose the class and the number of spots. If an app filled his class without his say, he isn't interested. Regulars must never see it as a special offer. |
| Q3 follow-up | Who would choose the spots | Him, always. He would also like to know who is new and who already comes, which he doesn't track today. |
| Close | Follow-up | Agreed to be contacted on WhatsApp if the team runs a trial. |

## 4. Key findings

- **Off-peak problem confirmed:** Friday 12:30 runs at about 25% capacity (2 of 8), with fixed instructor cost.
- **He keeps the class for loyalty:** cancelling would lose 2 regulars, so the empty seats are a recurring cost he accepts.
- **Discounting is ruled out:** he fears regulars will expect the lower price.
- **Open to the idea, with conditions:** owner control of seats and slots, and regulars never seeing the offer.
- **New need: new-vs-regular tracking.** He wants it but doesn't do it now.
