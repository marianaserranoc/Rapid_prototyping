# Opportunity

## PAIN POINT

A studio's recurring weekly off-peak class runs under capacity. Because rent and instructor pay are fixed regardless of attendance, every empty seat is money the studio loses outright, the cost structure alone means it would make sense to fill seats even at a steep discount. But discounting still doesn't solve the problem in practice: it tends to attract deal-hunters and cannibalize full-price regulars rather than recruiting net-new attendees who genuinely couldn't make that slot otherwise.

## USER

The owner/manager of a small, independent boutique studio running around 15–25 classes a week with a few instructors, where they personally set the schedule and watch attendance numbers class-by-class.

*Open gap: specific on operational size, but not yet on modality (yoga/pilates off-peak dynamics likely differ from CrossFit/HIIT) or city/geography, both probably shape why a given slot goes empty in the first place. Needs narrowing once interviews start.*

## NEED

Owners need a way to convert underfilled off-peak seats into predictable, steady attendance without resorting to discounting.

*Open gap: currently restates the HOW MIGHT WE rather than describing what owners have actually tried (discount codes, WhatsApp blasts, free trials, cutting the class) and why each falls short. Needs real owner interviews to become falsifiable.*

## INSIGHT (riskiest assumption — not yet validated)

Working hypothesis: demand for off-peak classes isn't absent, it's a timing mismatch — people who'd attend at a different time don't attend at this one, and discounting targets price sensitivity rather than the real constraint (timing).

Competing hypothesis, not yet ruled out: demand for this specific slot is simply absent — no one wants an 11am Tuesday Pilates class regardless of price or alternative timing.

These two hypotheses point to entirely different products (a rescheduling/matching mechanism vs. cutting or repurposing the slot), so this needs to be tested before being treated as settled. See `experiment-card.md`.

## HOW MIGHT WE

How might we help boutique studio owners convert underfilled off-peak seats into predictable, steady attendance without relying on discounting?

*Note: frames the problem around attendance/headcount as the owner's core complaint — worth confirming owners aren't actually more concerned with margin or instructor retention, which could point toward a different solution shape.*

## ONE SENTENCE

The owner/manager of a small, independent boutique studio needs to convert underfilled off-peak seats into predictable, steady attendance without relying on discounting — but whether this is a timing-mismatch problem or a genuine-absence-of-demand problem is still unvalidated and is the riskiest assumption going into prototyping.
