# Opportunity

## PAIN POINT
A studio's recurring weekly off-peak class runs under capacity. Because rent and instructor pay are fixed regardless of attendance, every empty seat is money the studio loses outright — the cost structure alone means it would make sense to fill seats even at a steep discount. But discounting still doesn't solve the problem in practice: it tends to attract deal-hunters and cannibalize full-price regulars rather than recruiting net-new attendees who genuinely couldn't make that slot otherwise.

---

## USER (Owner-side)
The owner/manager of a small, independent boutique studio running around 15–25 classes a week with a few instructors, where they personally set the schedule and watch attendance numbers class-by-class.

*Open gap: specific on operational size, but not yet on modality (yoga/pilates off-peak dynamics likely differ from CrossFit/HIIT) or city/geography, both of which probably shape why a given slot goes empty in the first place. Needs narrowing once interviews start.*

### NEED (Owner-side)
Owners need a way to convert underfilled off-peak seats into predictable, steady attendance without resorting to discounting.

*Open gap: currently restates the HOW MIGHT WE rather than describing what owners have actually tried (discount codes, WhatsApp blasts, free trials, cutting the class) and why each falls short. Needs real owner interviews to become falsifiable.*

---

## USER (Goer-side)
A studio-goer with flexible but non-habitual time who could plausibly attend an off-peak slot, but has not committed to a studio, package, or membership that would make that slot part of their routine.

*Open gap: unclear whether this is a prospective goer (never tried the studio), a lapsed member, or a current member who simply doesn't use off-peak slots — these are likely different people with different barriers, and interviews should distinguish them rather than treating "goer" as one segment.*

### NEED (Goer-side)
Goers need a way to try an off-peak slot or studio without bearing the full financial and psychological risk of committing before they know it's worth it.

*Open gap: same restatement problem as the owner-side NEED — needs real goer interviews describing what they've actually done instead (skipped the slot entirely, stuck to their usual class, gone to a competitor with lower commitment) and why.*

---

## INSIGHT (riskiest assumption — not yet validated)
Working hypothesis, connecting both sides: the barrier isn't timing or awareness — it's regret-aversion on the goer's side, which owners currently have no tool to address short of discounting (which backfires, per the PAIN POINT). Goers who could plausibly use an off-peak slot avoid committing because committing feels like an irreversible bet before they know the studio or slot is worth it. If this holds, the owner's NEED and the goer's NEED are two sides of the same unmet need: a way to let goers prove value before paying full commitment, which in turn lets owners fill seats without cheapening the brand.

Competing hypotheses, not yet ruled out:
- **Timing/awareness** (goer-side): demand exists but goers simply don't know or think to consider the off-peak slot at the moment they have a schedule gap.
- **Absence of demand** (goer-side): no one wants this specific slot regardless of price, timing, or risk — the slot itself, not the commitment structure, is the problem.
- **Owner-side risk aversion**: even if goer regret-aversion is real and a mechanism would work, owners may not adopt it because they see it as indistinguishable from discounting, or fear it cannibalizes existing members — this would block the venture even if the goer-side hypothesis is correct.

These hypotheses point to different products — a low-commitment trial/regret-proofing mechanism, a discovery/matching mechanism, or cutting/repurposing the slot entirely — and the owner-side adoption risk is separate from whether the goer-side mechanism would even work. Both need testing before either is treated as settled. See `experiment-card.md`.

## HOW MIGHT WE
How might we help boutique studio owners convert underfilled off-peak seats into predictable, steady attendance — by giving goers a way to prove an off-peak slot is worth it before committing — without relying on discounting or requiring owners to adopt something that feels like discounting?

*Note: still frames the owner's core complaint as attendance/headcount — worth confirming owners aren't actually more concerned with margin or instructor retention. Also explicitly folds in the owner-adoption risk (Option B's second USER) so the HMW doesn't solve only the goer's half of the problem.*

## ONE SENTENCE
Boutique studio owners need to convert underfilled off-peak seats into predictable attendance without discounting, and studio-goers need a way to try those slots without the risk of committing before they know it's worth it — but whether goer regret-aversion is the real barrier (versus timing/awareness, absence of demand, or owner resistance to adopting a non-discount mechanism) is still unvalidated and is the riskiest assumption going into prototyping.
