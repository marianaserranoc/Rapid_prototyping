# Persona hypothesis: The Uncommitted Last-Minute Booker

**Status:** Provisional Persona hypothesis requiring real-user validation
**Revision note:** Updated after independent AI cross-review — see changes below.

## Shared Job to Be Done

When she suddenly has free time, she wants to find and book a fitness class quickly so she can use that time without committing to something she's unsure about.

*(Kept intentionally distinct from the Persona below — the JTBD is the situation + motivation + desired progress; the Persona is the recurring behavioral pattern that shapes how she pursues it.)*

## Behavioural archetype

A young professional in Casablanca with an unpredictable schedule who discovers boutique fitness opportunities at short notice but has not committed to a specific studio or membership. When considering an unfamiliar off-peak class, she tends to hesitate when she lacks confidence in the experience or cannot complete the booking quickly.

## Decision-relevant profile

- **Relevant context:** Unpredictable, last-minute schedule; hasn't committed to a studio or package.
- **Most relevant current behaviour:** A cancelled meeting created free time; she saw an Instagram story for an unfamiliar off-peak class, considered booking it, DM'd the studio, got no quick reply, and abandoned the attempt — going for coffee, then doing a home workout instead. She reports this kind of situation happens "quite often."
- **Current approach or alternative:** Discovers classes via Instagram; books via DM/WhatsApp; sometimes leans on friends to make an unfamiliar studio feel safer; occasionally uses multi-studio apps.
- **Decision-relevant constraints:** She reports that the main concern is wasting money on an unfamiliar class, rather than the absolute price itself — this is her stated framing, not yet behaviourally verified.
- **Product or research decision affected:** Test whether reducing commitment risk (a low-commitment trial mechanism) actually increases completed bookings, after controlling for booking friction — rather than assuming regret-aversion is the primary problem to solve.

## Validation gaps

- Whether regret/uncertainty-aversion is really the dominant blocker, or whether booking friction, timing, or low information better explain the abandonment.
- Whether reducing commitment risk (vs. simply making booking faster) is what actually drives conversion — these are separable and currently conflated.
- Whether she would adopt Nova specifically, switch away from Instagram/WhatsApp, or pay for the service — all UNKNOWN, not yet tested.
- n=1: this is one respondent's account. It needs corroboration from more target users (10–15, per `assumptions.md`) before being treated as segment-representative.
