# Persona hypothesis: The Gambler-Averse Regular

**Status:** Provisional Persona hypothesis requiring real-user validation
**Side:** Goer
**Evidence base:** None. No interview, observation, or behavioral record. Built entirely from the team's working regret-aversion hypothesis in `opportunity.md`.

## Shared Job to Be Done

When a studio-goer with an existing fitness routine has their usual class slot become unavailable, the resulting forced binary go/no-go decision on a visible-but-unproven off-peak option prompts them to seek a way to protect their workout week without exposing themselves to unclear commitment risk or unresolved quality doubt about something they've never tried. *(See `jtbd.md`, goer-side.)*

## Behavioural archetype

A studio-goer who, when facing an unfamiliar off-peak class, treats booking as a bet rather than a neutral choice — pausing at the commitment decision, doing a quick low-effort gut-check that doesn't resolve fast enough, and defaulting to skipping rather than resolving the uncertainty.

## Decision-relevant profile

- **Relevant context:** Has an existing fitness routine and some flexible, non-habitual time in their week; not someone with totally open schedule flexibility.
- **Most relevant current behaviour:** When their usual slot is unavailable, checks the booking channel, sees the off-peak alternative is open, and pauses — booking means either a package or a steep one-off rate for something unproven. Does a quick, low-effort gut-check (Instagram, asking a friend) rather than real research; when that doesn't resolve fast enough, defers ("I'll try it another time") and skips.
- **Current approach or alternative:** Skips or falls back to a free, zero-risk default. Occasionally falls back to a known backup studio, or brings a friend — but these address different frictions (a workout in general; social risk), not the core commitment-risk barrier.
- **Decision-relevant constraints:** The cost of skipping (sunk membership cost, compounding streak loss, goal drift) is invisible in the moment of deciding and only registers retrospectively.
- **Product or research decision affected:** Whether the trial mechanism needs active-confirmation-gated charging rather than passive auto-charge; whether the "no-obligation" guarantee needs to be legible in-product rather than policy-based.

# Persona hypothesis: The Absorbing Owner

**Status:** Provisional Persona hypothesis requiring real-owner validation
**Side:** Owner
**Evidence base:** None. No interview, observation, or behavioral record. Built entirely through JTBD reasoning (owner-side) plus fresh inference — categorically thinner evidentiary base than the goer Persona (see Evidence boundary below).

## Shared Job to Be Done

When an independent boutique studio owner personally watches a recurring off-peak class run underfilled, class after class, the resulting real-time frustration — compounded by a past failed attempt at discounting — prompts her to seek a way to reliably fill that slot on terms she still controls, without retraining her clients to expect discounted pricing or requiring her to hand pricing and scheduling decisions to a system she doesn't fully trust. *(See `jtbd.md`, owner-side.)*

## Behavioural archetype

An independent studio owner who, when a recurring off-peak class runs underfilled week after week, doesn't resolve the problem or actively escalate against it — she absorbs it as an ongoing background cost, punctuated by occasional low-effort, low-expectation gestures (a social post) that don't interrupt the pattern.

## Decision-relevant profile

- **Relevant context:** Small independent studio, 15–25 classes/week, few instructors, personally sets the schedule and watches attendance class-by-class. Two distinct triggers operate on different cadences: a high-frequency, low-intensity live signal (watching the empty seats in real time, class-by-class) and a lower-frequency, higher-reflection signal (a forward-looking schedule/booking review).
- **Most relevant current behaviour:** No clean resolution point exists — unlike a discrete decision-and-close episode, the underfill is absorbed into an ongoing state rather than triggering a "try something / don't try something" moment each time. The class runs underfilled, she notices, nothing actionable happens live, and it becomes one data point folded into her next review.
- **Current approach or alternative:** Passive, ongoing absorption as the continuous baseline — not alternating between "acceptance" and "active effort" as two separate behaviors. Occasional low-effort gestures (a social post) occur *within* that passive state, not as escalation out of it; they don't appear to carry real expectation of working. Discounting was tried once, failed, and was abandoned — it is not current behavior. Cutting the class outright remains the one genuine exit from the pattern.
- **Decision-relevant constraints:** Likely time-constrained (defaults-acceptance under bandwidth pressure, not by disposition) in her general tool relationship — flagged as UNKNOWN and explicitly not to be assumed contaminated by her resignation on this specific problem. Control-attachment extends beyond pricing/scheduling to client data, brand presentation, and direct client communication — she will likely accept automation of low-stakes, reversible, backend mechanics, but wants a cheap, visible checkpoint before anything touches client-facing or brand-facing layers. Her failure threshold may be lower at first contact specifically — a resigned owner's prior ("nothing works for this") is broader and easier for any visible hiccup to confirm than a specific mechanism-fear would be — but this likely converges toward a higher, goer-like threshold after 1–2 visibly successful cycles.
- **Product or research decision affected:** Primarily, whether "proof it works" is a viable Pull mechanism at all, or whether a resigned prospect requires something that first interrupts her metabolized "nothing works" state before evidence can register. Secondarily, the "automate-the-mechanics, checkpoint-the-relationship" design split, and whether the first-contact experience needs to be near-flawless given a potentially lower initial failure threshold. Both secondary items are downstream of the primary resigned-vs-underserved question.

- **Highest priority:** is she genuinely resigned (has stopped believing anything will fix this) or under-served (would respond well to a working tool, just hasn't had one)? This is a strategic fork, not a framing detail — it determines whether the venture needs to solve a belief problem before an operational one.

- Whether a small first-time failure would confirm her general "nothing works" prior, or whether she'd extend patience as a one-off
