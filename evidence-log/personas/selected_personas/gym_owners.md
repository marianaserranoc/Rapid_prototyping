**Real sample: n=6 (A–F).** Five of six reject or have abandoned discounting (F uses it). No owner has committed a class on a date yet. AI enters only at the trial stage below, and those rows are not evidence.

## What this honestly supports, and what it doesn't

**Supports:** all 3 real owners, independently, said they'd consider the mechanism specifically because it keeps full price visible and lets them control which seats go out — that's a real, consistent, if small (n=3), finding worth reporting on its own.

**Does not support:** any specific conversion rate, any claim about the marketplace/discount framing being worse (only 1 real owner ever saw a framing comparison, and she wasn't shown the discount version), or anything from the AI-simulated rows treated as data about real owners.

Three real studio owners were interviewed and each responded positively, with conditions, to a full-price / owner-controlled mechanism. AI-simulated respondents were additionally used to rehearse the trial design before a larger real round; their answers are not treated as evidence.

**Update, 12 Oct:** the two new real owners (D, E) strengthen the "Supports" line above: now **5 of 5 real owners** reject discounting, and **4 of 5** would consider the full-price, owner-controlled mechanism (E: "maybe"). **13 Oct:** Owner F makes it 5 of 6 on discounting (her studio lowers early-class prices) and 5 of 6 on willingness. Owners D and E were not shown a trial card, so Stage 2 is unchanged.
