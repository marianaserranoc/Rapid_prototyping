# Persona hypothesis: The Price-Integrity Protector

**Status:** Selected critical Persona · grounded in 1 real owner interview (O1) · requires further real-user validation

## Shared Job to Be Done

Owner-side JTBD in `jtbd.md`, carried forward as context. **Alignment flag:** O1 still *sometimes* offers a discounted first class, so discounting is current behaviour, not only a past failed attempt as the JTBD states.

## Behavioural archetype

An owner-manager who sets the schedule, checks bookings the night before and the morning of each class, and prefers packages because they make revenue predictable — who has seen discounts bring people who don't return and make regulars question their price, and who therefore judges any off-peak offer by one test: does it keep the full price intact and bring people back.

## Decision-relevant profile

- **Relevant context:** ~20 classes/week, 4 instructors, classes of 8–12; regulars use a booking system, new clients arrive through Instagram/WhatsApp.
- **Most relevant current behaviour:** Knows a class will be quiet before it starts (Tuesday 11am, ~3 of 10); tries WhatsApp messages to lapsed clients and Instagram stories; sometimes offers a discounted first class; cut one class after months of low attendance.
- **Current approach or alternative:** Unpredictable WhatsApp/Instagram pushes, occasional first-class discount, and — as the exit — cutting the class.
- **Decision-relevant constraints:** Regulars notice and object to cheaper prices; packages valued for predictability; 5–6 hours/week already spent on manual WhatsApp admin; an extra attendee's value depends on the class and instructor cost.
- **Product or research decision affected:**
  1. The mechanism must **keep the full price visible** (risk reversal, not a price cut) — this owner explicitly separated the two.
  2. It needs **anti-abuse conditions** on refunds before this owner would offer it (design question for Area 3).
  3. The owner-facing success metric is **return visits of trial users**, not first bookings — that is what she said she'd need to see.
  4. It must not add manual WhatsApp work (refund handling, confirmations) — admin load is already the thing she would cut first.
