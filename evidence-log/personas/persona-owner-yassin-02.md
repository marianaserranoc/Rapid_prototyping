# Persona hypothesis: The Capacity-Protective Studio Owner

**Status:** Provisional Persona hypothesis requiring real-user validation
**Revision note:** Updated after independent AI cross-review — see changes below.

## Shared Job to Be Done

When a class is under-filled shortly before it starts, she wants to generate additional attendance so that otherwise-unused capacity produces revenue, without disrupting her normal business.

*(Kept intentionally distinct from the Persona below — the JTBD is the situation + motivation + desired progress; the Persona is the recurring behavioral pattern that shapes how she pursues it.)*

## Behavioural archetype

An independent boutique studio owner in Casablanca who personally monitors attendance and scheduling. When a class remains under-filled shortly before it starts, she wants to generate additional attendance from otherwise-unused capacity, but is cautious about anything that could reduce perceived value, disrupt existing customer relationships, or add operational work.

## Decision-relevant profile

- **Relevant context:** Personally manages or closely monitors the class schedule and attendance; hasn't spoken with Nova directly, so part of her account is expectation rather than lived experience.
- **Most relevant current behaviour:** Faced with a class with only two people booked, she ran it anyway rather than cancel, partly to avoid disappointing a regular customer — a specific recalled incident, not a general claim.
- **Current approach or alternative:** Uses Instagram, WhatsApp, and direct outreach/referrals to try to fill weak classes; some bookings and payments still handled manually.
- **Decision-relevant constraints:** She is reluctant to publicly lower the price of an under-filled class, because existing customers may notice and expect the same price — a specific decision mechanism, not a personality trait.
- **Product or research decision affected:** Test whether Nova can generate genuinely incremental attendance under conditions the owner controls (which classes, how many seats), without the mechanism reading as a discount to her existing customers — rather than assuming she has already decided to work with Nova.

## Validation gaps

- Whether other studio owners share these concerns, or whether this is specific to one owner's situation.
- **Incremental vs. cannibalized revenue** — whether a Nova-driven booking represents a genuinely new customer, a regular customer trading down in price, or someone who would have booked directly anyway. This materially affects Nova's economics and hasn't been tested.
- Whether owners would actually release inventory operationally (vs. liking the idea in the abstract), accept a commission structure, or keep using the platform after an initial trial.
- n=1: this is one respondent's account. It needs corroboration from more studio owners (5–8, per `assumptions.md`) before being treated as segment-representative.
