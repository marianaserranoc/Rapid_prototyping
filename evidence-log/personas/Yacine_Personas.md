# RPAI Personas: Off-Peak Class Attendance (Morocco)

*Two-sided personas for a product that helps boutique fitness studios fill off-peak slots without discounting. Market: Casablanca.*

> **How to read this document.** Both personas are set in Casablanca, the city with the densest boutique studio scene in Morocco. Prices are in Moroccan dirhams (MAD). Anything marked **[Assumed]** is a plausible guess, not a finding. Treat it as an interview question, not a fact. Cultural and seasonal details (Ramadan, summer, payment habits) are especially likely to need correction from local interviews.

---

## Market context: what's different in Morocco

**ClassPass isn't here, but its role is partly filled.** Local multi-studio apps already operate: Yofitt (monthly subscription with a capped number of visits, and a per-partner limit), Passmania (pay per session, and it advertises a refunded first session), and MGP (multi-studio, pay per session, no subscription). This matters for the project in two ways:

- The "no aggregator" gap is smaller than it looks. Owners and goers have likely already met the aggregator model, with its trade-offs.
- **Passmania's refunded first session is close to our regret-reduction idea.** Our mechanism can't just be "try without risk." It has to be specifically about *off-peak slots* and about turning a trial into *repeat attendance at the same studio*. [Assumed — check how these apps actually perform for studios and goers]

**Reference price:** A premium Casablanca studio (Encore Pilates) sells 5 classes for 1,500 MAD, about 300 MAD per class. That places boutique classes at the top end of the market, for a relatively small, affluent, urban clientele. [Assumed — the size of this segment is a key question for whether the market is big enough]

**Things that likely shape "off-peak" in Casablanca** [All assumed — validate]:
- **Ramadan** reshapes the whole timetable for a month: daytime classes empty out and post-iftar evening slots may become peak.
- **Summer (July–August):** many regular clients leave the city for the coast, so the whole schedule becomes "off-peak."
- **Traffic** makes location and timing matter more; a 15-minute distance can be 45 minutes at rush hour.
- **Booking and payment** often go through Instagram DMs, WhatsApp, cash or bank transfer (with a screenshot sent by WhatsApp) rather than card payment in a booking app. This adds friction to every spontaneous booking.
- **Women-focused spaces:** many boutique studios have mostly or only women clients, which affects trust and how new clients find a studio (often through friends).

## Persona 1: The Studio Owner

### Salma Bennani, 36, owner of Studio Nour (reformer Pilates & barre)

| | |
|---|---|
| **City** | Racine, Casablanca |
| **Occupation** | Owner and lead instructor of an independent reformer Pilates and barre studio, opened 3 years ago |
| **Background** | Worked 8 years in marketing at a multinational in Casablanca. Trained as a Pilates instructor in Paris during a career break, then opened the studio with savings and a family loan. |
| **Studio** | 8 reformers, one room, 20 classes per week, 3 part-time instructors plus herself. Clients are about 90% women. [Assumed] |
| **Fixed costs** | Rent about 25,000 MAD/month; instructors paid a flat 300 MAD per class regardless of headcount [Assumed — confirm rent levels and pay structures] |
| **Pricing** | Drop-in 280 MAD, 10-class pack 2,500 MAD, monthly unlimited 2,200 MAD [Assumed, based on the reference price above] |
| **Booking system** | A simple booking page for regulars, but most bookings still arrive by Instagram DM and WhatsApp. Many clients pay by bank transfer. [Assumed] |

### Weekly routine snapshot

- **Mon–Fri, 7:00am:** The 7:30am class is full with a waitlist, mostly women going to the office in Maârif and Casa Anfa afterwards.
- **Mid-morning:** Answers DMs and WhatsApp messages, confirms transfers, posts on Instagram, covers the 10:30am class if an instructor cancels.
- **Evenings:** 18:30 and 19:30 classes are 90–100% full. She teaches both twice a week.
- **Sunday evening:** Scrolls through next week's bookings and her WhatsApp threads to see who has confirmed. The off-peak problem hits her here, every week.
- **Saturday:** Teaches the 10am class, then family lunch, which she tries to protect.

### Off-peak picture

Peak slots (7:30am, 18:30, 19:30) run at 90–100%. Off-peak slots (Tue/Thu 10:30am, Mon/Wed 13:00, Fri 12:30, Sun 17:00) average 2–3 people out of 8. The Friday midday slot is weakest. [Assumed — ask owners for real occupancy by slot. This is the most important number to validate.]

### The concrete situation

On a Thursday in mid-September, the first week after most clients came back from the summer, the 10:30am barre class had two bookings. One was Mme Alaoui, a retired teacher who has come every Thursday since the studio opened. The instructor, Kenza, crosses the city from Aïn Sebaâ to teach it.

Salma thought about cancelling. It would save nothing, since she pays Kenza and rent either way. And it would upset Mme Alaoui, who tells all her friends about the studio. So she ran it, in a room with six empty reformers that she felt looked "sad" for a studio that is supposed to feel exclusive. After class, Kenza asked whether she should start looking for other morning work.

That evening, Salma considered offering the empty spots on a multi-studio app. She hesitated, because a friend who owns a yoga studio told her those users "come once and you never see them again." [Assumed — validate how often aggregator users in Morocco return]

### Current behaviour, step by step

1. **Sunday night review.** Checks bookings and WhatsApp confirmations. Any class under 4 gets a mental flag.
2. **Instagram story.** Posts "Il reste des places jeudi 10h30 ✨" with a nice photo. Most viewers are existing clients who already know the schedule.
3. **WhatsApp broadcast.** Messages her list of about 150 clients. She worries about messaging too often and seeming desperate.
4. **Asks regulars to bring a friend.** Friends come, but at the regular's usual time, which is usually peak.
5. **Multi-studio app listing.** Has listed or considered listing a few off-peak spots on a local app. Gets some bookings at a lower rate per visit; few return.
6. **Adjusts the schedule seasonally.** Cuts morning classes in summer and changes the timetable for Ramadan, largely by feel.
7. **Tried and abandoned:** A "morning offer" at 200 MAD for 10:30am classes. A peak-time regular noticed and asked for the same price. Salma quietly dropped it after three weeks.

### Frustrations

- "I pay for that class whether two women come or eight. The empty reformers are money I've already spent."
- Discounting clashes with the premium image she's built, and regulars notice and compare.
- Multi-studio apps bring one-time visitors, not *her* clients who come back and bring their friends.
- She can't tell whether nobody wants a 10:30am class or the right women simply don't know it exists. [Assumed]
- Managing bookings through DMs and transfers eats her mornings, the exact time off-peak classes need filling.
- Good instructors are scarce in Casablanca, and she's afraid of losing them if their classes stay empty. [Assumed]

### Hopes (in her words)

- "I want the quiet classes to have five or six regulars, so I can plan and keep my instructors."
- "I'd love new women to try us in the morning and then *stay*, not just come once because it was cheap."
- "I want to know if a slot is worth keeping before I cut it, especially before Ramadan and summer."

### The say-do gap

| She says | She actually does |
|---|---|
| "I don't discount. We're a premium studio." | Ran a cheaper "morning offer" and has considered listing spots on a multi-studio app at a lower rate. |
| "I'm organised about the schedule." | Decides slot changes by feel, by which regulars would complain, and by what the studio down the road does. |
| "Off-peak is my biggest problem." | Spends most of her marketing on peak classes and waitlists, which look good on Instagram. |
| "I want new clients." | Relies on word of mouth among existing clients and is wary of strangers from apps who don't know studio etiquette. [Assumed] |

**Why this matters:** She will probably reject anything that *looks* like a discount, even if it works economically. She will accept something that *feels* like an invitation or hospitality. In a market where trust spreads through friends and WhatsApp groups, how the mechanism is framed may matter as much as the mechanism itself.

### Existing alternatives she uses

| Tool or habit | Why it isn't enough |
|---|---|
| Instagram and WhatsApp broadcasts | Reach people who already know the schedule; risk seeming desperate. |
| DM and transfer-based booking | Adds friction to every last-minute booking, which is exactly what off-peak slots need. |
| Local multi-studio apps | Bring one-time visitors at a lower rate; feel like discounting by another name. |
| "Bring a friend" | Friends come at the regular's (peak) time. |
| Seasonal timetable changes | Done by feel; loses the few committed off-peak regulars. |

### USER → NEED → INSIGHT

> **Salma, an independent boutique studio owner in Casablanca,** needs **to fill her off-peak classes with new women who come back regularly, in a way that feels like an invitation rather than a discount,** because **she protects her premium image and her full-price regulars more fiercely than her revenue, and in a word-of-mouth market, a studio that looks cheap loses the clients who bring their friends.**

### Open gaps to validate in owner interviews

- Real occupancy by slot, and how seasonal patterns (Ramadan, summer) change what counts as off-peak.
- How owners currently experience local multi-studio apps, and whether they see our mechanism as the same thing.
- Rent levels and instructor pay, and whether an empty seat "costs" the same as in our assumptions.
- How much time owners spend handling DM and WhatsApp bookings, and whether booking friction is itself part of the problem.
- Whether the regulars' reaction to offers is a real constraint or the owner's fear of it.

## Persona 2: The Studio Goer

### Ghita El Idrissi, 28, freelance brand strategist

| | |
|---|---|
| **City** | Gauthier, Casablanca (15–40 minutes by taxi to Racine, depending on traffic) |
| **Occupation** | Freelance brand strategist for agencies and small Moroccan brands; works from home and cafés |
| **Income** | Irregular. Good months and lean months. Careful with money but not tight. [Assumed] |
| **Studio history** | Has taken around 10 reformer Pilates and barre classes over 18 months at 4 studios: mostly first sessions, a one-month multi-studio app subscription, and classes with friends. Enjoyed most of them. Has never bought a class pack. |

### Weekly routine snapshot

- **Weekdays:** No fixed hours. Some weeks she works 9am–8pm for a pitch. Other weeks she has whole mornings free, usually knowing only a day ahead.
- **Exercise:** Walks the Corniche with a friend twice a week in the early evening. Does YouTube Pilates at home when she feels she "should" be doing more.
- **Social:** Café meetings and dinners with friends several nights a week. Her friends' WhatsApp group is where most plans, including fitness, get made.
- **Weekends:** Often at the family home or at the beach in Dar Bouazza. Weekend classes at popular studios fill up early.

### The concrete situation

On a Thursday in September, a client cancelled their 11am meeting at 9:45am. Ghita suddenly had a free morning. She saw an Instagram story from a Racine studio: spaces left in the 10:30am barre class. Drop-in: 280 MAD.

She hesitated. What went through her head:

- "I've never been there. What if the class is advanced and I'm the only one who doesn't know what she's doing?"
- "Why are there so many spots? Is this the class no one likes?"
- "280 dirhams for one class, at a time I probably won't have free next week. It won't turn into anything."
- "I'd have to DM them, wait for a reply, and send a transfer. By then it'll be too late."

She sent a DM. No reply after 20 minutes. She went for a coffee instead, and later did a YouTube video. That afternoon she spent around 150 MAD on lunch and coffee with a friend without thinking twice. [Assumed — our reading is regret-aversion, but the DM delay and short notice may have been the real barrier. Validate by asking goers to walk through a recent "almost booked" moment.]

### Current behaviour, step by step

1. **Notices she's free.** Usually on the same day or the day before.
2. **Checks Instagram.** Studios she follows sometimes post open spots. Peak classes are full; open slots feel like a gamble.
3. **Tries to book.** Sends a DM or WhatsApp. If the reply is slow or requires a transfer first, the moment often passes.
4. **Looks for a safe option.** Waits for a friend to come with her, or looks for a first-session offer at a new studio, because a first session feels lower risk.
5. **Uses fallbacks.** Walks on the Corniche, YouTube Pilates, or nothing.
6. **Tried and abandoned:** A one-month multi-studio app subscription. She enjoyed trying different studios but used fewer visits than she'd paid for, felt she'd "wasted" the money, and didn't renew. [Assumed]

### Frustrations

- "I don't mind paying for a class. I mind paying for a class I end up not liking."
- Classes she'd feel confident about are full; the ones she *can* get into feel risky.
- Monthly subscriptions and packs assume a routine she doesn't have.
- Booking through DMs and transfers makes spontaneous classes nearly impossible. [Assumed]
- Going alone to a new studio feels awkward; going with friends means coordinating schedules that never match.

### Hopes (in her words)

- "I want to try a class and, if it's not for me, not feel like I've thrown money away."
- "I'd love a studio where I could just show up on my free mornings and feel at home."
- "I want something that fits my weeks as they really are, not as I pretend they'll be."

### The say-do gap

| She says | She actually does |
|---|---|
| "I really want to be consistent with Pilates." | Tries first sessions at different studios and never commits to one. |
| "It's the price. Studios in Casa are so expensive." | Spends a similar amount on cafés and dinners without hesitation. The problem may be the fear of waste, not the amount. [Assumed — key hypothesis to test] |
| "I'd prefer smaller, calmer classes." | Books busy classes when she can, and avoids emptier off-peak slots because an empty class looks like a bad sign. |
| "My schedule is too unpredictable." | Is free most weekday mornings in a typical month, but decides day by day, so no routine forms. [Assumed] |
| "I don't need anyone to go with me." | Almost all her studio visits so far were with a friend. [Assumed] |

**Why this matters:** Lowering the price alone may not work, because the barrier may be the risk of regret, social uncertainty, or simple booking friction, not the cost itself. And an empty off-peak class may send a hidden signal: "this is the lesser option."

### Existing alternatives she uses

| Tool or habit | Why it isn't enough |
|---|---|
| First-session offers | Good for one visit; the next one is full price and the risk comes back. She moves on to the next studio. |
| Multi-studio app subscription | Monthly commitment and unused visits created the "wasted money" feeling she wants to avoid. |
| Instagram stories and DMs | Show her open spots, but booking is slow and needs a transfer. |
| Going with friends | Makes a new studio feel safe, but coordinating schedules rarely works. |
| Corniche walks and YouTube | Free and flexible, but no equipment, no instructor, no sense of belonging. |

### USER → NEED → INSIGHT

> **Ghita, a flexible-schedule goer in Casablanca who likes studio classes but hasn't committed anywhere,** needs **to try an unfamiliar studio's off-peak class quickly and without feeling she's made a bet she might regret,** because **the fear of paying for a class that turns out wrong weighs more on her than the price itself, and an emptier class, booked through a slow DM, reads as the lesser option.**

### Open gaps to validate in goer interviews

- Whether regret-aversion is really the barrier, versus awareness, booking friction, or simply no demand for midday classes.
- Whether "empty class = bad class" is a real signal goers pick up on.
- How far ahead flexible-schedule goers decide to exercise, and how Ramadan and summer change that.
- How goers feel about multi-studio apps, and what made them stop or continue.
- What would make a trial feel regret-free: a refund, credit toward a later class, coming with a friend, more information about the class, or something else.
- Whether goers who try an off-peak slot once would come back to *the same* slot, which is what the owner needs.

---

## How the two personas connect

Salma won't lower the price, and Ghita's real problem may not be the price. If Ghita's barrier is regret rather than cost, a mechanism that reduces *risk* could fill Salma's seats without looking like a discount. But in Morocco two things sharpen the test: local apps already offer risk-free first sessions, and booking friction may be a barrier as big as regret. The first interviews should try to disprove the regret-aversion hypothesis, and separate it clearly from booking friction and lack of awareness.
