# Interview Evidence

This shared document collects interviews conducted by the team and their main takeaways, so the project can refer back to traceable evidence. Gym-goer interviews come first; gym-owner interviews follow in a separate section.

Only the interview supplied for this document is included so far. Other project files contain research summaries; these have not been imported or treated as additional interviews here.

## How to contribute

- Add each interview under the appropriate participant group, using a unique ID (`GOER-002`, `OWNER-001`, etc.). Do not overwrite previous interviews.
- Include the interviewer, participant code, interview date, location or market, relevant participant context, and transcript or detailed notes. Mark missing information as **Not provided**.
- Use participant codes rather than personal identifying information. State whether the transcript is translated, edited, or verbatim.
- Separate reported behavior, stated preferences, hypothetical intent, and interviewer interpretations. Support takeaways with references to numbered exchanges.
- Update the relevant group's main takeaways as evidence accumulates. Record contradictions and sample limitations rather than forcing agreement.

## 1. Gym Goers

### Main takeaways across gym-goer interviews

**Evidence base: one interview (GOER-001).** These findings describe this participant and are not yet generalizable to other gym goers.

| Main takeaway | Supporting evidence | Implication and limits |
|---|---|---|
| Interest in variety can coexist with satisfaction with a current gym. | GOER-001, exchanges 2, 5, 7, and 9. | Occasional complementary classes may be more relevant than replacing the existing membership. No actual purchase of an external class was reported. |
| An existing prepaid membership and familiar routine can discourage exploration. | GOER-001, exchanges 6 and 8. | The alternative competes with a service already paid for and a familiar travel and training routine. The interview does not isolate the relative weight of each factor. |
| Memberships offer perceived value and simplicity, despite the risk of unused time. | GOER-001, exchanges 3–5 and 9. | Flexibility alone may not outweigh lower per-class costs and less payment tracking. The participant identified travel as a potential source of waste, without describing a specific loss. |
| Machines and friends contribute to the value of the current gym. | GOER-001, exchange 9. | A class-only offer would address only part of this participant's training needs and motivations. |
| Lower-priced individual classes could encourage occasional exploration. | GOER-001, exchange 9; interviewer note. | This is hypothetical intent prompted by a price scenario, not evidence of conversion, switching, or willingness to pay a specific amount. |

### GOER-001 — Satisfied gym member seeking occasional variety

#### Interview details

| Field | Details |
|---|---|
| Interviewer | S (full name not provided) |
| Participant | B |
| Participant context | B attends a gym with machines and scheduled classes available to members, including spinning and GAP (glutes, abs, and legs). B reports attending four days per week and having paid for three months. |
| Fit to research | Useful evidence about a satisfied existing gym member's interest in complementary classes. Fit to the project's geographic target and to customers of specialist studios is unconfirmed. |

#### Interview transcript — English translation

**1. Current training routine**

**S:** Tell me about the last time you had a free hour and thought about doing a class. What happened? What do you usually do?

**B:** Every weekday. I either use the machines or, if I like the classes available at the gym, I attend a class.

**2. What is missing?**

**S:** Do you feel that anything is missing from your gym?

**B:** More variety. I would like to try other gyms or studios with different classes from time to time.

**3. Membership experience**

**S:** Have you ever bought a package or membership at the gym? What was your experience like?

**B:** I usually pay for several months. It works well for me. I go four days a week, so I make good use of it.

**4. Downsides of paying in advance**

**S:** Is there anything negative about paying for a month or a package of classes?

**B:** If you travel or something like that, you end up losing money.

**S:** Is that the only downside?

**B:** Yes.

**5. Reasons to try other classes**

**S:** Why would you like to try other classes?

**B:** For a change. The gym offers certain things, but other places can offer different things. Still, I think booking through the gym is worth it because it is cheaper than an individual class.

**S:** So it is worth it, but you would like a little more variety.

**6. Barriers to trying another venue**

**S:** What stops you from trying another gym or a center offering another sport?

**B:** I have paid for three months at this gym. Also, the effort of trying another place and learning to use different machines. I am already used to how long it takes me to get there.

**7. Satisfaction and switching**

**S:** Do you like your current gym?

**B:** Yes.

**S:** If you did not like it, would you leave?

**B:** I would change gyms.

**8. Current need to change**

**S:** So what also holds you back is that you do not really need to change right now.

**B:** Yes.

**9. Hypothetical individual-class pricing**

**S:** Imagine another gym or sports studio that you like offered individual classes at the same per-class price as a membership or package. Would that change anything?

**B:** I would stay at my gym because I am happy there and I also like the machines. You do not have to keep checking how many days you are paying for or how much it all adds up to. But I would take those classes from time to time for a change. Ultimately, I am not only looking for classes: I like the machines, and my friends are at my current gym, which motivates me to train.

#### Interviewer's post-interview note

**S's interpretation, translated and summarized:** Other studios are substantially more expensive than B's current gym, which B is happy with. S considers B outside those studios' current target customer profile. S suggests that, if trying classes were less expensive—for example through ClassPass—B might eventually join another gym.

**Evidence boundary:** This note is the interviewer's interpretation. B directly expressed interest in occasional additional classes while keeping the current gym. B did not state that ClassPass would cause a switch, and no ClassPass use or subsequent purchase was reported.

#### Interview-specific conclusions

- **Reported behavior:** B pays for multiple months and attends four days per week, combining machines with appealing scheduled classes (exchanges 1 and 3).
- **Stated preference:** B wants occasional variety while remaining satisfied with the existing gym (exchanges 2, 7, and 9).
- **Stated barriers:** The prepaid membership, effort of adapting to another venue, and familiar journey discourage exploration (exchange 6). The interviewer additionally identifies higher studio prices in the post-interview note.
- **Stated value:** Lower per-class membership costs, payment simplicity, machines, and friends help retain B at the current gym (exchanges 5 and 9).
- **Hypothetical intent:** At membership-equivalent per-class prices, B would consider occasional external classes while keeping the current gym (exchange 9).
- **Project interpretation:** A complementary discovery offer may fit this participant better than a replacement membership. This remains a hypothesis to test through actual bookings.

#### Limitations and follow-up questions

- The opening question did not produce a specific recent episode; most answers describe general habits or preferences.
- Some prompts are leading, particularly the summary about having no need to change and the hypothetical price scenario. Agreement should be interpreted cautiously.
- Prices, class types B wants to explore, actual attempts to book elsewhere, and the frequency of desired external classes were not established.
- No actual external booking, attendance, switching, or willingness to pay a specific price was observed.
- Follow up on the most recent occasion B considered an external class: venue, price, steps taken, and final decision. Confirm interview date and location as well.
