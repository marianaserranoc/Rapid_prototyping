# Jobs to Be Done hypothesis

**Status:** Working hypothesis requiring real-user validation

## Job to Be Done

When a studio-goer with an existing fitness routine has their usual class slot become unavailable, the resulting forced binary go/no-go decision on a visible-but-unproven off-peak option prompts them to seek a way to protect their workout week without exposing themselves to unclear commitment risk or unresolved quality doubt about something they've never tried.

- Whether the retrospective cost (streak loss, goal drift) actually changes next-week behavior, or simply registers and fades with no behavioral consequence
- Whether "known backup studio" and "bringing a friend" ever genuinely compete against skip/fallback in the same decision moment, or only ever appear in separate, unrelated circumstances
- Whether financial/commitment anxiety and quality/fit anxiety are genuinely separable in a real person's mind, or whether people experience them as one bundled concern (test with two separate questions, not one)
- Whether skipping feels bad at all in the moment of deciding, even mildly — this would confirm or kill the "weak push" reading
- Whether the fallback default is a genuine preference or purely a path-of-least-resistance choice — these imply different-strength interventions

# Jobs to Be Done hypothesis — Owner-side

**Status:** Working hypothesis requiring real-user validation

## Job to Be Done

When an independent boutique studio owner personally watches a recurring off-peak class run underfilled, class after class, the resulting real-time frustration — compounded by a past failed attempt at discounting — prompts her to seek a way to reliably fill that slot on terms she still controls, without retraining her clients to expect discounted pricing or requiring her to hand pricing and scheduling decisions to a system she doesn't fully trust.

| Lens | Element | Working definition | Basis or status |
|---|---|---|---|
| How the job begins | Circumstance | Owner/manager of a small independent boutique studio, running roughly 15–25 classes a week with a few instructors, who personally sets the schedule and watches attendance class-by-class | Team interpretation |
| How the job begins | Trigger | A hybrid trigger: anticipatory (seeing an underfilled slot coming during a schedule/booking review) combined with a sharper, more repeatable real-time trigger — being physically present and watching the empty seats during or right after the class itself | Team interpretation |
| Progress | Desired progress | Primarily functional: durable, compounding predictability she can plan instructor pay and scheduling around. Emotional relief (the in-room frustration easing) is a downstream signal that the functional job is done, not a separate goal in itself. Reputational/brand protection (not looking discount-driven) functions as a constraint on which solutions are acceptable, not as progress being sought. | Team interpretation — open question whether "predictability" means revenue certainty, scheduling/staffing certainty, or both |
| Progress | Solution hired | Passive acceptance (absorbing the financial loss) as the likely dominant current default, given no evidence of a systematic, repeatable response in the available grounding. Discounting is reclassified as a past, documented, failed attempt — not current ongoing behavior. Active-but-weak gestures (asking regulars to bring friends, posting on social media) are sporadic and low-consistency, not a real competing solution. Cutting the class outright is kept distinct as exiting the job entirely, rather than tolerating it unsolved. | Team interpretation |
| Forces compelling change | Push | Likely stronger in raw terms than the goer-side equivalent — acute, real-time, recurring on a tight class-by-class cycle rather than delayed or diffuse. However, flagged as carrying a real risk of having normalized into learned resignation through repeated, unresolved exposure (especially following the failed discounting attempt), which would look identical from the outside but function as a much weaker force. | Team interpretation, flagged as the single highest-priority open question |
| Forces compelling change | Pull | Three candidates, unequal standing: (1) Proof it's genuinely different from discounting — reclassified as a gate/disqualifier, not a positive pull on its own. (2) Proof it would actually work — entangled with the resignation risk in Push; if resignation is real, this functions as a precondition for re-engaging Push rather than a classic pull. (3) Ease/low setup effort for a time-constrained solo operator — least contaminated candidate, but reclassified as a hygiene factor rather than a differentiator, since it would apply to any solution offered to her. This entire lens could not be properly checked for circularity against the actual product concept, since the owner-facing mechanism has not yet been defined in this work. | Team interpretation, low confidence — structural gap named |
| Forces opposing change | Habit | Reclassified as control/autonomy-attachment rather than path-of-least-resistance or laziness — grounded in her personally setting the schedule and watching attendance herself. Explicitly distinguished from loyalty to her current methods (likely weak, given those methods appear inconsistent and largely passive) and from trust built with existing regulars (likely to transfer to any new offering rather than being tied to the current broken process). Flagged as a real risk of over-reading a single circumstantial detail into a larger conclusion than the evidence supports. | Team interpretation |
| Forces opposing change | Anxiety | Split into three distinct forces, since each resolves via a different lever: (1) Control-loss anxiety — fear that a system will act on her behalf (pricing, scheduling, terms) without her approval; resolved via visible control/approval mechanisms. (2) Reputational anxiety — fear of training regulars to expect discounted terms or of the studio appearing less premium; resolved via discretion/framing of the offer; this is the only one of the three with direct grounding, tracing back to the documented discounting failure. (3) Financial/terms anxiety — uncertainty about fees, revenue-share, or payout; resolved via transparent, predictable terms. Operational/bandwidth risk ("can I manage one more thing") was explicitly excluded from Anxiety and reclassified under Habit/adoption-friction, since it reflects effort rather than a felt sense of danger. | Team interpretation — only the reputational anxiety is grounded in existing material; the other two are inference without a supporting document |

**Relevant progress dimensions:** Functional (primary). Emotional and reputational dimensions were both reclassified — emotional as a downstream signal of unmet functional progress, reputational as a constraint on acceptable solutions rather than progress itself.

### Validation gaps

- Whether Push is genuinely acute and live, or has normalized into resignation after repeated unresolved exposure and the failed discounting attempt — highest priority to resolve, since it changes whether the product can lead with "we'll fix this" or needs to first re-establish that the problem is solvable at all
- The actual owner-facing mechanism has not been defined anywhere in this work — without it, Pull cannot be properly checked for contamination with the product concept
- Whether control-loss is a real, load-bearing objection to adoption, or an over-read of a single circumstantial phrase ("personally sets the schedule")
- Whether the three split Anxieties (control-loss, reputational, financial/terms) are genuinely distinct concerns to a real owner, or would bundle back together under direct questioning
- Whether "predictability" as desired progress means revenue certainty, scheduling/staffing certainty, or both, and whether they carry equal weight
