# Jobs to Be Done — Goer side and Owner side

**Status:** Working hypotheses, updated 12 Oct 2026 after sprint feedback: two goer circumstances instead of one, Pull and Anxiety marked explicitly as hypotheses, card-trial support removed, and real evidence added (G4, G5, Owners D and E, A/B reservation test).

**How to read the Basis column:**
- **Interview**: said by a real person we spoke to (goers G1, G2, G4, G5; Owners A–E). See `user-feedback.md`.
- **Test**: what real people did in the A/B reservation test (`ab-test-results.md`): reserved or not, and the reason they ticked.
- **Desk research**: public sources (`Fermi_estimate.md`).
- **Team interpretation**: our reasoning, not yet backed by evidence.
- **HYPOTHESIS**: a force we believe acts on the decision but have not yet observed directly. Pull and Anxiety are hypotheses by nature: they explain why people would switch, which interviews can only suggest.
- *Removed:* "Trial" (card trials in `oz-trials.md`). They were illustrative examples, not real data.

# Goer side

## Two circumstances, one job
Real interviews show two different ways into the same job, so we keep both rather than forcing one trigger.

| | Circumstance A: the unexpected free window | Circumstance B: the friend's invitation |
|---|---|---|
| What happens | A meeting is cancelled or a morning frees up; there are minutes to decide | A friend keeps inviting them to try a new format (reformer); the decision is postponed for weeks |
| Who | G1 (free morning), G2 and G4 (cancelled meeting → Instagram story → DM) | G5 (friend asking for months; she keeps saying "maybe next weekend") |
| What kills it | Slow confirmation (G2, G4) or the already-paid gym (G1) | Fear of committing to a pack before knowing she'll like it; beginner nerves (G5) |
| What it needs from us | Instant confirmation, one class, no pack | One class, no pack, at full price, and something that shows what the class is like |

## Job statement
**When** I have a reason to try a studio class I don't know yet (a free window that just opened, or a friend who keeps inviting me), **I want to** book one class without locking into a pack before I know it's right for me, and know straight away that my spot is confirmed, **so I can** try it without wasting money on classes I won't use or time on a booking that never comes through.

| Lens | Element | Working definition | Basis |
|---|---|---|---|
| How the job begins | Circumstance | An active person with a default way to train (often a gym membership) and no commitment to a studio. **A:** flexible or unpredictable time. **B:** a friend already goes to a studio | **Interview** G1, G5 (gym members); G2, G4 (unpredictable schedule, no studio) |
| How the job begins | Trigger | **A:** an unexpected free window (G1 free morning; G2 and G4 cancelled meeting, then an Instagram story). **B:** a friend's repeated invitation to a new format (G5) | **Interview** G1, G2, G4 (A); G5 (B) |
| Progress | Desired progress | Functional: try a class that might be worth it, without wasting money compared with what they already pay | **Interview** G1, G5 (price vs. gym; "one class I'd lose is fine, five up front is a different story") |
| Progress | Solution hired | Fall back to the already-paid default (G1 went to the gym), give up when booking stalls (G2, G4), or keep postponing (G5). Studios also sell low-commitment entry: Core Studio's 3-class "Discovery pack" (600 MAD, one month) | **Interview** G1, G2, G4, G5; **Desk research** |
| Forces compelling change | Push | Weak in the moment: skipping costs nothing visible. For B, social pressure from the friend builds slowly | **Team interpretation**; consistent with G1 choosing the gym and G5 postponing for months |
| Forces compelling change | Pull | **HYPOTHESIS.** (1) **No pack:** being able to try one class without prepaying. (2) **Certainty:** a spot confirmed straight away. (3) **Price parity:** one class that doesn't feel expensive next to the gym. (4) **A friend going too** (B only) | (1) **Supported, not proven:** G1, G4, G5 say it; in the **Test**, removing the pack lifted reservations from 28% to 64%. (2) Suggested by **Interview** G2, G4, who lost bookings to slow replies; not tested. (3) Suggested by **Interview** G1, G5 and 10 of 27 **Test** declines (price or gym); unresolved. (4) Suggested by **Interview** G5 only |
| Forces opposing change | Habit | The already-paid default needs no decision, no new place, no new instructor | **Interview** G1, G5 (gym members); 5 of 27 **Test** declines said "prefer my gym" |
| Forces opposing change | Anxiety | **HYPOTHESIS.** Four anxieties, each solved differently: (1) **Prepaid waste:** buying a pack and not using it; solved by no-pack entry. (2) **Booking certainty:** not knowing if the spot is theirs; solved by instant confirmation. (3) **Not knowing the class:** whether they'll like it and what it's really like; solved by information (videos, coach) or a friend. (4) **Beginner discomfort:** being the only one who doesn't know what to do | (1) **Interview** G1 (used 5–6 of a 10-pack), G4 (unused membership), G5 (friends' unused sessions); top **Test** decline reason (10 of 27). (2) **Interview** G2, G4; 1 of 27 **Test** declines, but the test held it constant. (3) **Interview** G5 ("Instagram doesn't show the experience"); G1 named the instructor as the reason to return; 2 of 27 **Test** declines. (4) **Interview** G5 only |

**Progress dimensions:** Functional (primary). Circumstance B adds a **social** dimension (trying something with a friend, not feeling like the only beginner), raised by G5 only, so it stays a hypothesis.

### Validation gaps
| Gap | How we will check it |
|---|---|
| Does the no-pack pull still hold when people must actually turn up and pay? | Phase 2 pilot: attendance and payment at full price |
| Is "no pack" about the number of classes or the 900 MAD paid up front? | Split A/B decline reasons by arm; ask in pilot follow-ups |
| How often does Circumstance B (friend invitation) happen vs. A (free window)? | Ask every pilot booker how they heard about the class |
| Are the four anxieties separate in people's minds, or one bundled concern? | Ask about each separately in pilot follow-ups |
| Does the price barrier hold for people without a gym membership? | Record membership status for every pilot participant |
| Does showing the class (video, coach profile) reduce anxieties 3 and 4? | Test a short class video on the booking page in Phase 2 |

# Owner side

## Job statement
**When** I watch a recurring off-peak class run with a few people in it, week after week, **I want to** fill those seats with new people at my normal price, choosing myself which seats are offered, **so I can** cover rent and instructor pay without my regulars learning to expect discounts.

| Lens | Element | Working definition | Basis |
|---|---|---|---|
| How the job begins | Circumstance | Owner or manager of a small independent boutique studio (15–25 classes a week) who sets the schedule and checks bookings personally | **Interview** Owner A (checks bookings the night before and the morning of); Owner E (yoga and mobility, ~16 classes a week, sets the schedule himself) |
| How the job begins | Trigger | A specific recurring quiet slot, known by name and number: Tue 11:00 with about 3 of 10 (Owner A); Fri 12:30 with 2–3 of 8 (Owner C); weekday 11:00 and lunch with 3–4 of 8 (Owner D); Fri 12:30 with 2 of 8, because of mosque and family lunch (Owner E) | **Interview** Owners A, C, D, E |
| Progress | Desired progress | Functional: predictable revenue. Owner A prefers packages because they make revenue predictable. Protecting the brand from looking discount-driven is a constraint on acceptable solutions, not the goal itself | **Interview** Owner A, B, C; Owner D (a newcomer is valuable only if they become a regular) |
| Progress | Solution hired | Mostly passive acceptance: running a near-empty class rather than cancelling (Owner B ran a 2-person class so a regular wasn't disappointed), or cutting the class after months of low attendance (Owner A). Owner E keeps a 2-person class running for 2 regulars. Instagram posts and stories bring a few bookings, not consistently (D, E). Discounting was tried and abandoned (A, C) or ruled out (B, D, E). Some studios sell discovery packs to new clients | **Interview** Owners A–E; **Desk research** (Core Studio) |
| Forces compelling change | Push | Acute and recurring: all four owners asked named the exact slot and headcount; one has already cut a class over it; others pay the instructor for a near-empty room every week | **Interview** Owners A, C, D, E |
| Forces compelling change | Pull | **HYPOTHESIS.** (1) Seats filled at full price, so regulars see no discount. (2) Proof it works before committing (Owner A: "wants to see it work first"; Owner D: test on a couple of classes). (3) Knowing which bookers are new vs. existing, and whether they come back | Suggested by **Interview**: (1) A–E; (2) A, D; (3) C, D, E all want it and none track it today. Not yet observed: no owner has released a real seat |
| Forces opposing change | Habit | Attachment to control over the schedule and seats, not inertia. Four of five owners made seat control a condition of saying yes; Owner E: "him, always" | **Interview** Owners B, C, D, E |
| Forces opposing change | Anxiety | **HYPOTHESIS.** (1) **Reputational**: regulars notice and expect the same deal. (2) **Control loss**: a third party or app deciding which seats go out. (3) **Wasted effort**: newcomers who come once and vanish. (4) **Financial terms**: fees, revenue share, payouts | (1) Suggested by **Interview** A–E (all five). (2) **Interview** B, C, D, E. (3) **Interview** D. (4) **Team interpretation**, not yet raised by any owner |

**Progress dimensions:** Functional (primary). Emotional relief is treated as a signal the job is done; reputation as a constraint on solutions.
