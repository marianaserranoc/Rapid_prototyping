# Goer side

## Job statement
**When** an unexpected free window opens in my week (a cancelled meeting, a free morning), **I want to** turn it into a workout at a studio class I haven't tried, without paying more than my default or locking into a package before I know it's worth it, **so I can** keep my training on track without wasting money or time on a bad fit.

| Lens | Element | Working definition | Basis |
|---|---|---|---|
| How the job begins | Circumstance | An active person with a default way to train (often a gym membership, 3–4× a week) and some flexible, non-habitual time | **Interview** G1 (gym member, trains 3–4×/week); G2 (unpredictable schedule) |
| How the job begins | Trigger | **An unexpected free window**, not a full or cancelled usual class as the previous version assumed. G1 had a free morning that matched a slot; G2's meeting was cancelled and she saw a studio's Instagram story | **Interview** G1, G2.|
| Progress | Desired progress | Functional: use the free window for a workout that is worth the money compared with what they already pay for | **Interview** G1 (single class felt expensive vs. the gym already paid for); retrospective streak loss  stays **Team interpretation** |
| Progress | Solution hired | Fall back to the already-paid, zero-decision default (G1 went to the gym) or give up when booking stalls (G2 abandoned after no quick reply). Studios also sell their own low-commitment entry: Core Studio's 3-class "Discovery pack" (600 MAD, one month) | **Interview** G1, G2; **Desk research** (Core Studio price list) |
| Forces compelling change | Push | Weak in the moment: skipping costs nothing visible at the time of deciding | **Team interpretation**; consistent with G1 choosing the gym without hesitation |
| Forces compelling change | Pull | (1) A single-class price that matches what they already pay per gym visit. (2) A booking that is visibly confirmed straight away. (3) Removing commitment risk (refund, no package) — the original hypothesis, now the weakest of the three | (1) **Interview** G1 + **Trial** Persona 1 (5 of 8). (2) **Interview** G2 + **Trial** Persona 2 (instant confirmation 4 of 5 vs. refund 2 of 5). (3) **Trial** Persona 3 (5 of 7) **[G3 — confirm source]** |
| Forces opposing change | Habit | The already-paid default needs no decision, no new place, no new instructor | **Interview** G1 |
| Forces opposing change | Anxiety | Three separate anxieties, each solved differently: (1) **Booking certainty** — not knowing if they actually have a place; solved by instant confirmation. (2) **Prepaid waste** — buying a package and not using it (G1 used 5–6 of a 10-pack); solved by no-package entry. (3) **Quality / instructor fit** — not knowing if the class is good; solved by information or social proof | (1) **Interview** G2 + **Trial** Persona 2. (2) **Interview** G1. (3) **Trial** Persona 1 (1 of 3 decliners); G1 named the instructor as the main reason to return |

**Progress dimensions:** Functional (primary). Emotional and social dimensions were considered and ruled out as unsupported.


### Validation gaps
| Gap | How we will check it |
|---|---|
| Is commitment risk a real barrier once price and certainty are fixed? | The two-arm primary test (`experiment-card.md`) |
| Are the three anxieties separate in people's minds, or one bundled concern? | Ask about each separately in the post-test debrief |
| Does the price-parity finding hold for people without a gym membership? | Record membership status for every primary-test participant |
| Does instructor familiarity act as its own lever? | Debrief question: "would knowing the instructor have changed your decision?" |
| Does skipping feel bad at all in the moment? | Debrief question on how they felt when they skipped last time |

# Owner side

## Job statement
**When** I watch a recurring off-peak class run with a few people in it, week after week, **I want to** fill those seats with new people at my normal price, choosing myself which seats are offered, **so I can** cover rent and instructor pay without my regulars learning to expect discounts.

| Lens | Element | Working definition | Basis |
|---|---|---|---|
| How the job begins | Circumstance | Owner or manager of a small independent boutique studio (15–25 classes a week) who sets the schedule and checks bookings personally | **Interview** Owner A (checks bookings the night before and the morning of) |
| How the job begins | Trigger | A specific recurring quiet slot, known by name and number: Tue 11:00 with about 3 of 10 (Owner A); Fri 12:30 with 2–3 of 8 (Owner C) | **Interview** Owner A, Owner C |
| Progress | Desired progress | Functional: predictable revenue. Owner A prefers packages because they make revenue predictable. Protecting the brand from looking discount-driven is a constraint on acceptable solutions, not the goal itself | **Interview** Owner A, B, C |
| Progress | Solution hired | Mostly passive acceptance: running a near-empty class rather than cancelling (Owner B ran a 2-person class so a regular wasn't disappointed), or cutting the class after months of low attendance (Owner A). Discounting was tried and abandoned (A, C). Some studios sell discovery packs to new clients | **Interview** Owner A, B, C; **Desk research** (Core Studio) |
| Forces compelling change | Push | Acute and recurring: owners can name the exact slot and headcount, and one has already cut a class over it | **Interview** Owner A, C |
| Forces compelling change | Pull | (1) Seats filled at full price, so regulars see no discount. (2) Proof it works before committing (Owner A: "wants to see it work first"). (3) Knowing which bookers are new vs. existing (Owner C wants this but would not build it herself) | **Interview** Owner A, B, C |
| Forces opposing change | Habit | Attachment to control over the schedule and seats, not inertia. Two of three owners made seat control a condition of saying yes | **Interview** Owner B, C |
| Forces opposing change | Anxiety | (1) **Reputational** — regulars notice and expect the same deal (all three). (2) **Control loss** — a third party deciding which seats go out (B, C). (3) **Financial terms** — fees, revenue share, payouts | (1) **Interview** Owner A, B, C. (2) **Interview** Owner B, C. (3) **Team interpretation**, not yet raised by any owner |

**Progress dimensions:** Functional (primary). Emotional relief is treated as a signal the job is done; reputation as a constraint on solutions.


### Validation gaps
| Gap | How we will check it |
|---|---|
| Does the push hold across more owners, or did we find the three most motivated? | 2–5 more owner interviews (`recruitment.md`) |
| Why don't existing discovery packs already fill off-peak classes? | Ask in the next owner interviews |
| Is financial-terms anxiety real? | Show owners a concrete commission or revenue-share and record reactions |
| Does "predictable" mean revenue, staffing, or both? | Ask owners to rank them |
| Is new-vs-existing tracking a must-have or a nice-to-have? | Ask in the seat-release walk-through (`open-gaps.md`, Gap 9) |
