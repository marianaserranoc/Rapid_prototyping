# Assumption Map — Nova

Three MECE areas covering the logic that has to hold for Nova to work: users have to want it (Demand), studios have to supply it (Supply), and the economics have to hold together (Business Model).

## Area 1: Demand — Users will subscribe and actually use it
- **Must be true:** Enough young professionals starting a fitness habit will pay a recurring subscription (ClassPass-style credits) rather than sticking with one gym membership or booking studios directly for free.
- **Must be true:** The variety of trying different studios is valuable enough to these users that they'll pay a premium for flexibility, not just the cheapest option.
- **Evidence needed:** Problem interviews with 10–15 target users in Casablanca on current booking frustration and willingness to pay; a landing page or waitlist testing actual sign-up intent, not just verbal interest.

## Area 2: Supply — Studios will partner and stay stocked
- **Must be true:** A meaningful number of boutique gyms/studios in Casablanca (beyond Yassin's existing personal contacts) will agree to open part of their booking calendar to Nova.
- **Must be true:** Studios will accept giving up some pricing/schedule control and a revenue split, in exchange for the extra bookings Nova brings — without this being undercut by studios pushing users to book direct instead.
- **Evidence needed:** Direct conversations with 5–10 studio owners/managers (starting with Yassin's existing contacts, then at least a few cold) to confirm interest, acceptable commission range, and what "enough new bookings" would need to look like for them to say yes.

## Area 3: Business model — The economics hold together
- **Must be true:** The subscription price Nova can charge users is high enough to cover what studios need to be paid per class, plus payment processing and operating costs, while still leaving Nova a margin.
- **Must be true:** Enough classes get booked per subscriber per month that studios see it as worthwhile volume, not occasional freeloading of empty slots.
- **Evidence needed:** Rough numbers from a few studios on their per-class value/margin tolerance, plus local payment processing fee rates in Morocco (e.g. CMI, local mobile payment options), to sanity-check a subscription price point.

## Priority

**Riskiest assumption: Supply — that studios will partner with Nova (Area 2).**

**Why:** Yassin already has personal contacts at a few studios, but that's a small, warm sample — not evidence that studios broadly will hand over calendar control and a revenue cut to a brand-new, unproven app. Nova is a two-sided marketplace: without enough studio variety on the app, there's nothing for users to subscribe to, so this is the assumption most likely to break the idea before demand or pricing even get tested. It's also the one Yassin himself flagged as the biggest worry.

## Open gaps
- No studios have been asked yet to actually commit calendar access or a commission rate — the "warm contacts" are relationships, not confirmed partners.
- The subscription price point and commission split are undecided; Area 3 numbers are placeholders until real conversations happen.
- Demand evidence (Area 1) has not been collected from anyone outside Yassin's own thinking yet.
- Casablanca-specific payment and market conditions (competing apps, local gym pricing norms) haven't been checked yet.
